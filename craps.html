Craps Hints

How a round works · best bets · bets to skip

How a round works

  1. Come-out roll. The puck says OFF. Bet the Pass Line, then roll. 7 or 11 wins right away; 2, 3 or 12 (“craps”) loses. Any other number — 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 or 10 — becomes the point.
  2. The point is set. The puck flips to ON over that number. Now keep rolling until one of two things happens:
  3. The point rolls again → Pass Line wins. Or a 7 rolls first (“seven out”) → Pass Line loses. Every other number in between doesn't matter to the Pass Line.
  4. After either one, a new come-out roll starts.

Notice the switch: on the come-out a 7 is your friend, but once a point is set the 7 is what beats you.

The best play

Bet the Pass Line, then take max odds once a point is set. Tap the Pass Line again after the point to add odds behind it. The odds bet pays true odds, so it has no house edge at all — the more of your money sits in odds instead of the flat bet, the better.

This table allows 3-4-5× odds: up to 3× your Pass bet when the point is 4 or 10 (pays 2:1), 4× on 5 or 9 (pays 3:2), 5× on 6 or 8 (pays 6:5). Every one of those works out to a win of 6× your flat bet. With full odds every time, the house edge on everything you bet drops to about 0.37%.

Want more action? Make Come bets — each one works like a fresh Pass Line bet on the next roll — and take odds on those too. Bet sizes that divide cleanly help: on 6/8 odds use multiples of $5, on 5/9 use even amounts, or payouts get rounded down.

Pass or Don't Pass?

Don't Pass is a hair better (1.36% vs 1.41%, and about 0.27% with full lay odds), but you're betting against everyone else at the table and laying more than you win on the odds. Either is a good choice — Pass is the friendlier, more common one.

Remember: no bet in craps beats the house over time. Low-edge bets just make your bankroll last longer.
